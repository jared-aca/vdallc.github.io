VA GERD Ratings After the 2024 Digestive-System Changes

GERD received its own diagnostic code in 2024. Learn why the current criteria focus on esophageal stricture and why older rating decisions need careful review.

GERD received its own diagnostic code in 2024. Learn why the current criteria focus on esophageal stricture and why older rating decisions need careful review. The outcome of a VA disability claim depends on the specific evidence, the governing regulation, and the procedural history. This guide explains the practical questions to examine before deciding what to file.

Why the date of the rating criteria matters

VA revised the digestive-system rating schedule effective May 19, 2024. GERD now has its own Diagnostic Code 7206 under 38 C.F.R. § 4.114. Older GERD awards and appeals may involve the pre-amendment schedule, often by analogy to hiatal hernia under former Diagnostic Code 7346. The applicable criteria depend on the claim's procedural history and the rules governing regulatory changes.

It is inaccurate to tell every veteran that heartburn, regurgitation, or medication automatically qualifies for a particular current GERD percentage. The revised criteria use a different framework.

What current Diagnostic Code 7206 focuses on

The current GERD criteria evaluate documented esophageal stricture, including dysphagia, and the treatment needed to address it. The schedule distinguishes levels involving daily symptoms controlled by medication, dilation or stenting, recurrent or refractory strictures, and more serious interventions. The exact rating requires applying the full regulatory text to the clinical record.

An upper endoscopy, barium swallow, specialist evaluation, or documented intervention may be important depending on the claimed severity. Symptoms alone should be described accurately, but they are not a substitute for the specific findings required by the current code.

GERD symptoms still matter clinically

Reflux, burning pain, sour regurgitation, nighttime choking, difficulty swallowing, and weight or nutrition changes should be reported to treating clinicians. They help explain the course of the condition and may lead to evaluation for esophageal complications. However, rating eligibility and medical treatment decisions are different questions.

Do not stop prescribed treatment to try to demonstrate worse symptoms at a C&P examination. Document what happens despite treatment, including any breakthrough symptoms or complications.

Practical takeaway: Before choosing a review option or submitting more evidence, compare the rating decision with the records VA actually considered. The most useful next step addresses the precise missing legal or factual element.

Older claims and the former analogy to hiatal hernia

Before the amendment, GERD was often evaluated by analogy under former Diagnostic Code 7346, which considered combinations of symptoms and impairment of health. For a claim spanning the regulatory change, the effective date and applicable version of the schedule can affect the analysis. The newer criteria should not simply be projected backward to periods before their effective date.

A review should identify the exact period at issue, the rating decision date, and which version of the criteria VA applied. An appeal about a 2022 rating can raise different questions from a new claim filed in 2026.

Evidence for an accurate evaluation

Gather gastroenterology notes, diagnostic studies, medication history, swallowing complaints, documented strictures, dilation or stenting records, and any treatment for complications. Explain how symptoms affect eating, sleeping, and daily activities without assuming those descriptions alone determine the schedular percentage.

Where the records show a serious swallowing problem, a qualified clinician should address its cause rather than automatically attribute every symptom to GERD.

What to do after an unexpected GERD rating

Compare the decision's diagnostic code and cited criteria with the version of 38 C.F.R. § 4.114 applicable to the relevant period. Review whether the examiner obtained the information the code requires. If the decision overlooks relevant records or applies the wrong standard, determine whether timely review, additional evidence, or an increase claim is appropriate.

Because this area changed recently, generic online charts copied from pre-2024 articles can be misleading. Always verify the regulatory version and date.

Questions to ask before you file

  • What exactly did VA concede in its favorable findings, and what element remains disputed?
  • Which evidence was already in the record when VA made the decision?
  • Is the issue a legal or factual mistake, missing development, or a genuine lack of supporting evidence?
  • What is the date of the decision notice, and which review deadline applies?
  • Would the proposed evidence answer the specific reason VA gave for its decision?
